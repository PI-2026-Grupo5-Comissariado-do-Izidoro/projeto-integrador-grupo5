The Chronicle

The Battle

The dust was still in the air when the battle ended. The sound of the soldiers shouting gradually faded until an unsettling silence fell. Near a destroyed wall, an enemy soldier lay on the ground, wounded and unable to move. He was unarmed and no longer posed any threat.

One of the officers approached and ordered the young soldier to kill the enemy. The soldier took a few steps and aimed his rifle at the man on the ground. But when he looked at him, he did not see a monster or someone who could still attack. He saw a frightened young man, with a torn uniform and fear in his eyes.

The officer repeated the order, this time more firmly. The young soldier stood still, unable to fire. He knew he could get into trouble for disobeying a superior, but he also knew that killing someone who had already surrendered was a terrible act of cowardice. At that moment, he had to choose between obeying his superior's order and doing what was morally right.

"He has already surrendered and is wounded. He can no longer fight," the soldier said, lowering his weapon. The officer was angry at the young man's actions, but another soldier decided to help and called the medical team to care for the prisoner. The man was then taken for treatment and placed under military guard.

Years later, the soldier could barely remember the medals he had received or the strategies he had learned during the war. Even so, he never forgot that day. He realized that human rights and citizenship exist not only in books or laws, but also in our actions. Respecting another person's dignity matters even when we are in a difficult situation and it seems that everyone around us thinks differently.
